Before you read

An approved lesson is immutable. The store refuses an edit to one — a change means writing a new lesson that supersedes it. So approving is the expensive direction, and rejecting costs nothing but the row.

Approving is not the same as agreeing a finding happened. These sentences were written by the agent, not by you. Selecting a finding is not approving the wording it was written up in.

  • Approve and pin — binds every future session and is written into the note the agent loads at startup. Reserve it for the ones worth interrupting someone with.
  • Approve — binds, but stays out of the startup note.
  • Reject — the row stays as history and binds nothing.
  • Refine — tell me what is wrong with the sentence and I will rewrite it for another pass.

The lesson

THE INSTANCE. `WBS-40.1` was written on 2026-09-08 from `DW-069`, and its DONE-CLAIMED-WHEN clause asserted a check that `DW-069` spends four hundred words proving cannot exist: *"a check can show no term has two Arabic renderings in the locale bundles"*. The source row records that 55 of 76 such divergences are Arabic gender and definiteness agreement and are **CORRECT** — `Cancelled` renders as `مُلغى`, `ملغى` and `ملغاة` across three surfaces and all three are right — so collapsing them would be the bug, not the fix. Re-measured 2026-09-09: **77 of 1,098 labels**, essentially unchanged. **A check of the literal clause reports 77 failures over a correct codebase.**

THE SHAPE, AND IT IS NOT A DUPLICATE OF THE TWO NEAREST LESSONS. `LL-052` says read an ARTEFACT to its end before saying what it contains. `LL-010` says open the AUTHORITY a requirement names before writing an instrument for the conformance half. **This is neither: the row WAS read, and by an author who plainly had it open, because the item is a faithful precis of it in every other clause.** What went wrong is that compressing a long row into a short done-claim **silently drops the row's HEDGES** — and a hedge is precisely the part a done-claim must keep. `DW-069`'s conclusion is *undecidable*; the item's compression of it is *decidable, and here is the check*. **The derivation inverted the source's own finding while preserving every fact in it.**

WHY NOTHING MECHANICAL CAN SEE IT, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES IT WORTH A ROW. Every identifier in `WBS-40.1` resolves. Its lifecycle status is right. It is bound to the correct slice and phase. `gate_run` was **7/7** and `readiness_check("package")` was **`ready:true`** on the exact tree that carried it. `G-IDS` checks foreign keys; `G-COMPLETE` screens for placeholder tokens; **no rule compares a derived row's CLAIM against the row it was derived FROM**, and none could without reading both for meaning. The item reads as ordinary, competent scoping right up until you measure it.

What to do instead

**WHEN A PLAN ROW (`WBS-`, `AC-`, a slice objective) IS DERIVED FROM A REGISTER ROW, RE-READ THE SOURCE ROW TO ITS END AT THE MOMENT YOU WOULD ACT ON THE DERIVED ONE — not when you wrote it — and check specifically whether the source states a LIMIT the derivation dropped.**

The tells are cheap and they are all words the source uses about itself: *undecidable*, *not adjudicable by a reader of code*, *candidate set rather than a finding set*, *must not be reported as*, *cannot be closed by*, *do not let a later session*. **`DW-069` contains four of those six.**

**If the derived row asserts something the source explicitly says cannot be asserted, THE DERIVED ROW IS WRONG AND THE SOURCE IS NOT.** Be sure of that direction: the derived row is newer, shorter and looks more actionable, which is exactly why it wins arguments it should lose.

⭐ THE COROLLARY, WHICH SAVES TIME RATHER THAN COSTING IT: **this is what the pre-build sweep is FOR, and it works.** The sweep is the scheduled moment when the source gets re-read against the derivation. It has now STOPPED an item twice — `WBS-26.4`, where `DW-091`'s central claim was a misattributed quotation, and this one — and resized four more. ⛔ **Do not treat the sweep as ceremony to be shortened when an item looks well-specified; an item that looks well-specified is the case this lesson is about.**

Why it generalises

Every mechanical check this package owns operates on identifiers, statuses, foreign keys and token patterns. A derivation error preserves all four perfectly — it is a claim about MEANING, made in prose, inside a row whose every column is correct. That is why the failure survives a green board, and why the only instrument that reaches it is a human re-reading the source at the moment of action. Recording it as a lesson rather than leaving it inside `DEC-156`'s rationale matters because the decision row explains one instance, while the pattern applies to every `WBS-` and `AC-` this project derives from a register row — which is most of them.

If followed

An item whose done-claim cannot be satisfied is caught before any code is written, at the cost of re-reading one row. The two instances so far each stopped an item that would otherwise have produced either a check reporting failures over correct code, or a reversal of an explicit operator decision.

If ignored

A session builds to a done-claim that cannot be met, and discovers it only after writing the check and watching it report dozens of failures over code nobody should change — at which point the tempting repair is to weaken the check rather than the claim, which enshrines the inversion in a committed instrument.
