Before you read

An approved lesson is immutable. The store refuses an edit to one — a change means writing a new lesson that supersedes it. So approving is the expensive direction, and rejecting costs nothing but the row.

Approving is not the same as agreeing a finding happened. These sentences were written by the agent, not by you. Selecting a finding is not approving the wording it was written up in.

  • Approve and pin — binds every future session and is written into the note the agent loads at startup. Reserve it for the ones worth interrupting someone with.
  • Approve — binds, but stays out of the startup note.
  • Reject — the row stays as history and binds nothing.
  • Refine — tell me what is wrong with the sentence and I will rewrite it for another pass.

The lesson

WHEN YOU SWEEP FOR *WHAT WAS DECIDED*, YOU SWEEP DECISIONS, ADRs AND OPEN QUESTIONS - AND A REAL DECISION MAY BE SITTING IN A `Fixed` DEFECT ROW, WHICH READS AS A CLOSED PROBLEM RATHER THAN AS A RULING. Measured in this package: **10 of 113 closed defect rows record an operator decision**, including one - `DEF-079` - that a later work item was scoped to REVERSE without anyone noticing, because every sweep for a governing decision came back empty. **A row's lifecycle status tells you the problem is closed; it tells you nothing about whether the row also holds a ruling that still binds.** And the same rows' PROSE is not maintained after closure: a status is corrected when the work lands, the surrounding sentences usually are not.

What happened

WBS-26.4 was scoped to switch compose dependents from service_started to service_healthy, on DW-091's claim that a misleading comment had concealed an oversight. The pre-build sweep found the opposite: service_healthy had been declared, its total-outage consequence measured, put back to the operator, and reversed by their choice - all recorded in DEF-079, a Fixed defect row. Sweeping decisions, ADRs and open questions for the trade returned nothing. Generalising the class then found that 10 of 113 closed defect rows carry operator decisions, and that two of them (DEF-094, DEF-031) carry PROSE that is now false while their statuses are correct: DEF-094 still offers disposition options for a disposition long taken and says no AC exists when AC-122 is Approved and Met; DEF-031's open_gap says backup failures have no alerting path when backup.sh publishes to SNS from an EXIT trap.

What to do instead

Add closed rows to the *what was decided* sweep, not only open ones - `Fixed`, `Won't-fix` and `Obsolete` defects, and closed deferred-work rows. Grep them for decision language (*put back to the operator*, *the operator chose*, *operator decision*) as well as for the identifier you care about. ⚠ **Read the hits rather than counting them: tense is invisible to a regex.** *"Needed an operator decision"* followed by *"✅ DECIDED"* is history, not an open question - two of the ten flagged that way and both were clean. And when a row's status changes, **re-read its prose in the same batch**: a status is one column and the sentences around it are another, and nothing in the store compares them.

Why it generalises

It is LL-038's family - a state can be load-bearing and its reason may live where no register view looks - with the specific, checkable location this package keeps using. LL-038's instances have now been a progress entry, no record at all, a code comment, a closed defect row, and stale prose inside a correctly-statused row; this names the fourth and fifth and gives them a sweep. It also inherits LL-006's rule (read the thing itself) with a warning attached: the sweep that finds these rows CANNOT adjudicate them, because the discriminator between a live question and a narrated one is grammatical.

If followed

A work item is never scoped to reverse a decision nobody could find, and a closed row's prose is corrected when its status is. The sweep for governing decisions covers where decisions actually are, rather than where the schema suggests they should be.

If ignored

An item gets activated, decided and scheduled on a premise a closed row already refuted - which is exactly what happened to WBS-26.4, costing a scope change and a blocked build - and closed rows accumulate prose that reads as open work, inviting sessions to re-do settled things or to re-litigate settled ones.
