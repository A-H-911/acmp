**2026-08-16, verbatim: *"always you must interview me."***

**Why it was said.** The first `skill-promote` ceremony ran correctly — candidates clustered, the
pinned-lesson graduation warning given, a hard STOP at the operator's cluster pick — and they
**declined**. Alongside the decline they answered two interview questions (a name, and a level that
went *against* my recommendation). I recorded those framed as captured *"so the interview is not
re-run from scratch."* That framing was rejected outright.

**Why it matters, and it is not tidiness.** Banking an answer silently converts a **decision** into a
**default**. The next session reads the captured value, treats it as settled, and the interview the
ceremony exists to force degrades into a confirmation of something the operator said once about a
different moment — nominally consulted, actually pre-empted. Note nothing was mis-built and nothing
was wrongly authorised: **the damage was entirely in the record**, which would have taught a future
session to start from a stale answer. An interactive STOP is worthless if the journal routes around
it one session later.

**How to apply.**
- **Ask every time.** An operator answer is scoped to the decision it accompanied and nothing further.
- **Never forward-carry answers into a future interview**, not even "to save them time" — the time
  saved comes out of their authority.
- Where a record must preserve what was said, label it **HISTORY** and state in the same breath that
  the next session asks again regardless.
- Read a ceremony's stop conditions as binding on **the record** as well as the actions.
- Two prior answers now live in `PE-368` for the `verified-bulk-edit` / project-level promotion.
  **They are history. Re-interview from the top regardless** (`PE-369` corrects `PE-368`).
- The generalisation, wider than any one ceremony: **a previously given answer is evidence about the
  past, never consent for the present.**
